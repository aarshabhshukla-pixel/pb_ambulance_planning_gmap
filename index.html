    Speeds from TomTom traffic

    free, no card

    Asks TomTom for drive times with its usual traffic for each period on the next weekday, between your hospitals / bases and points across the zone. Sets each period's speed and the road circuity. Uses about 48 route lookups; TomTom's free allowance is 20,000 a month.

    Speeds from Google traffic

    needs Google (paid)

    Asks Google for drive times with predicted traffic between your hospitals / ambulance bases and points across the zone, at each period's midpoint on the next weekday. Sets each period's speed and the road circuity from Google's answers. Uses up to 1,000 route lookups.

    Calibrate speeds from real trips

    Paste trips whose driving time you know, from ambulance GPS logs, past calls, or Google Maps directions at that time of day. One per line: from lat, from lng, to lat, to lng, minutes, and optionally the period (am / day / pm / night) or the hour. Without a period, a trip counts toward the selected one.

    Response standards

    NAS 2026 defaults

    From the National Ambulance Service guidelines (June 2026): 20-min call-to-scene standard, 10-min goal in busy urban areas, dispatch under 3 min, at least 95% of the fleet available.

    Zone & deployment band

    Ambulance types

    AIS-125

    A first responders (incl. bikes for congested lanes) reach the scene but don't transport. B patient transport is non-emergency and is left out of response figures. Response uses A, C and D; hospital transport uses C and D.

    Costs (₹)

    own fleet only

    Per ambulance. Fixed per month = crew for all shifts + maintenance + insurance + parking. Running per km = fuel and wear.

    Ola Maps

    Not set up

    Road distances and drive times for India (check Ola’s current free monthly limit). Times are for right now: Ola can't predict traffic for a future day or hour, so every day and shift gets today's traffic. Create a key at maps.olakrutrim.com, then put it in config.js as OLA_MAPS_API_KEY, or paste it here for this browser only.
